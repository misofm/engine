# Citation policy

## Purpose

This policy keeps design claims traceable and separates DSP evidence from product-workflow observations.

## Source classes

1. **Primary standards and papers** support equations, measurement methods, and numerical claims.
2. **Official specifications and vendor manuals** support platform, protocol, routing, automation, latency, and product-workflow claims.
3. **Scholarly texts** support established derivations when an accessible paper or standard is not the appropriate source.
4. **Secondary sources** may orient research but cannot be the only support for a production DSP or acceptance claim.

## Required citation practice

- Use bibliography keys in square brackets, for example `[RBJ-COOKBOOK]`.
- Link each key to a stable official, author-hosted, publisher, DOI, or standards page in `BIBLIOGRAPHY.md`.
- Paraphrase. Do not copy extended source text.
- Every DSP topic uses at least two primary or official sources when such sources are available. If there is only one appropriate primary source, say why in the note's `Sources` section.
- Vendor manuals establish observed interface patterns only. They never establish DSP transparency, sound quality, or numerical accuracy.
- Paywalled material may be cited for bibliographic provenance; do not assert uninspected normative detail from it. Use an accessible official preview or author manuscript for the actual claim.

## Decision traceability

Each adoption must name (a) the observed/source-backed fact, (b) the V2 choice, and (c) a measurable reason or test that can falsify it. “Industry standard” and subjective praise are insufficient reasons.

## Citation review gate

An issue fails citation review if it has an unsupported numerical claim, treats a manufacturer manual as DSP proof, cites a source not in the bibliography, or presents a source as evidence for a fact it does not cover.
